Jetwire: a VPN designed with AI for modern threats

Most VPNs still do one job: hide your IP address. Today's risks are leaked passwords, stolen sessions and tracking that follows your email. I used AI to design a VPN that handles those too, then rebuilt it by hand into a prototype you can try below.

How I designed it with AI

I started with a brief, not a blank canvas. I described the product to Google Stitch, and in a few hours it gave me seven screens and a design system: dark surfaces, cyan for activity, emerald for safety.

Then I did the part AI doesn't do: I tested it. The screens looked finished but broke in use. I kept the visual direction, fixed what was wrong, wrote all the copy and rebuilt every screen in React, so each button does something real.

The challenge

Legacy VPNs were built for one threat: someone watching your Wi-Fi. Now your password can leak in another company's breach, a stolen cookie can sign in as you, and every sign-up form asks for your real email. A VPN that only hides your IP address misses all of that.

The goal

A VPN that protects the person as well as the connection. You can see at a glance whether you're safe. It warns you about leaks and helps fix them, keeps your real details out of forms, and lets you check its claims yourself.

What the AI got wrong

Before
After
  • Broken logo. The image is missing on every screen, so the header shows its alt text instead.
  • Crowded header. The protocol pill runs into the “Protected” badge.
  • Cut-off data. Server and city names are truncated: the details people check most.
  • Monospace capitals everywhere. Labels, buttons and data all look the same.
  • Copy that overclaims. “Military-grade”, “untraceable”, a “synthetic billing address” for tax purposes. I rewrote or removed all of it.
  • No grid, missing states. Uneven spacing, no connecting, disconnect or failure states, and splash text that overlaps.

Key flows

01 · Connect

Protected or not, at a glance

The ring has three states: grey when you're exposed, cyan while it connects, green when you're protected. Your real IP and the IP sites see sit side by side, so “protected” means something concrete.

02 · Kill switch

A safety net you can watch working

Drop the network and the app turns amber, holds all traffic and marks your IP as paused. When the network comes back, it reconnects and confirms that nothing leaked.

03 · Multi-hop route

More privacy, with the delay shown up front

Four modes, each with a one-line trade-off. The route is drawn from your phone to the exit server, and the estimated delay updates with every hop you add.

04 · Data leak

The VPN tells you your password leaked, then helps fix it

A legacy VPN wouldn't notice this. Jetwire shows what leaked and how risky it is, then offers a fix plan: three steps it can do for you and one only you can do. The password is never shown.

05 · Alt ID

Aliases, so forms never get your real details

Legacy VPNs hide where you are. Alt ID also hides who you are, with email and phone aliases and a one-time card that needs your fingerprint to reveal.

06 · Privacy audit

Proof you can run yourself

Five leak tests run while you watch. Run them while disconnected and three fail, and the app explains why. A check that can fail is easier to trust than a badge that always says A+.

A new logo

The AI used a lightning bolt, which plenty of other apps already use. I drew a J from two parallel wires, the tunnel, with a white dot inside it, the data. It stays readable at 16 px.
