The World

A Buddhist temple stands at the top of a long mountain stair. Its great gate, the niōmon, is watched by the two Niō: the vajra-holding guardian kings, the Kongō rikishi who give the game its name.

They stand watch over everyone who climbs to the gate. When a pilgrim proves worthy, a storm gathers, the bronze guardians wake, lightning falls from the vajra and the gate opens its treasure.

Agyō opens his mouth on “A”, the first sound. Ungyō closes his on “Un”, the last. Together, A-Un: the beginning, the end and everything between.

Game Background
The Guardians

Two kings, one pair. They are always powerful and dignified, never comic or cute. On the reels they are high symbols; in free spins each one grows into a full-reel wild panel.

Agyō Mouth open in a roar, the sound “A”, the beginning. Raises the gold vajra. Fierce, all motion.
Ungyō Mouth closed, the sound “Un”, the end. Raises an open palm to halt evil. Silent resolve.
The Vajra The guardians’ thunderbolt weapon: unbreakable like a diamond, unstoppable like lightning. It spins on every big moment.
The Ryū Japanese three-claw dragons, guardians of the temple’s heaven. One for each win tier.

Symbols & Paytable

Every symbol is a solid carved coin with no holes or cut-outs, so the set reads as one material. High coins have a gold rim and a sakura vine, low coins a plain bronze rim and their own plate shape. Pays are × bet per line for 3 / 4 / 5 of a kind, placeholders tuned for a convincing demo.

Style Guide

Carved Edo-period kinko metalwork, like a tsuba: high relief (takabori), gold inlay (zōgan), dark patinated shakudō with nanako dot texture. Soft key light from the top left, museum-photo realism.

  • Every asset is carved metal: symbols, world, dragons, UI and even the lightning.
  • Symbols are solid coin-like medallions: no gaps, the relief fills the face.
  • A limited palette: metal, gold, bronze, copper and sakura. No neon.
  • Sayagata pattern only in the background, at low contrast.
  • Lettering: engraved serif capitals on carved metal plaques, Latin letters only.
  • Authentic details: three-claw ryū, zuiun clouds, kaen flames, Kamakura-era Niō.
Respect Rules

The game borrows from a living religious tradition, so the lore came with rules that every image and line of code had to follow.

  • A Buddhist temple gate (niōmon), never a Shinto torii.
  • No Buddha figure anywhere: not a symbol, a character or a jackpot.
  • No kanji or fake Japanese script. AI draws fake kanji, so all text is in Latin letters.
  • No manji symbol, no sutra text, no real temple names.
  • Guardians and dragons always powerful and dignified, never comic, cute or sexualised.
  • Before showing it to a Japanese audience: one native-speaker review.

Design Notes
  • Heavy, not neon. Easing, blur, landing and sound are tuned to feel like carved metal: lift, thud, bronze bell, no bleeps.
  • Only the rotor turns. The SPIN button’s lighting is baked into the art, so only its inner tomoe disc rotates, and a counter-gradient cancels the turning highlight.
  • The art stays untouched. Ungyō faces the other way in the art, so he is mirrored in code; the bet panel is cropped, not squashed, to fit.
  • Tonal sound only. Rain, wind and an electric crackle were tried and cut as noise. The base game is quiet except for a distant bell; free spins add distant thunder.
  • No jumping plaques. Win tiers crossfade between layers of a fixed size, so the plaque never resizes as the counter climbs.
  • Demo maths. An outcome-first engine picks the result, then builds reels that pay it, simulated over many spins to hit the target rates.
  • Made in code. Paylines, gold rims, glows, ripples, coin and petal particles, the logo glint and every sound.
  • Plain stack. HTML, CSS and JavaScript only, no frameworks, no build step, no audio files.
